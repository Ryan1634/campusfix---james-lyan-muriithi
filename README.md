CampusFix Problem Statement

1. Introduction:
Universities use many facilities and equipment every day. These include classrooms, laboratories, computers, projectors, lighting systems, water systems, doors and furniture. When any of these develops a problem, it needs to be reported and fixed so that learning and other university activities can continue normally.
CampusFix is a proposed system for reporting and managing these maintenance problems in a more organized way.

2. The Problem:
At the moment, a student or staff member who notices a problem may report it verbally, through a phone call, WhatsApp message, email or by contacting someone in the maintenance department directly.
The main problem with this approach is that there is no single place where all the maintenance requests are recorded and followed up.
For example, a student may report that a projector in a classroom is not working. The person receiving the complaint may forget about it, fail to forward it to the right maintenance worker, or the student may not know whether anything is being done about it.
This makes it difficult for the university to know which problems have been reported, which ones are urgent, who is working on them and how long they have taken to resolve.
CampusFix is intended to solve this problem by providing one system where maintenance problems can be reported, assigned, updated and eventually closed.

3. Stakeholders:
The main people who would interact with or benefit from CampusFix are:

Students
Students can report problems they find in classrooms, laboratories and other university facilities. They can also check the progress of their reports.

Teaching Staff
Lecturers and other teaching staff can report problems that affect teaching, such as faulty projectors, computers, lights or classroom equipment.

Maintenance Staff
Maintenance workers receive assigned requests and update their progress as they work on the reported problem.

Maintenance Supervisor
The supervisor reviews incoming requests, decides which problems need more urgent attention and assigns work to maintenance staff.

System Administrator
The administrator manages users and has access to system management functions.

University Management
Management can use information from the system to understand common maintenance problems and monitor how maintenance work is being handled.

4. Examples of Problems CampusFix Can Handle:
Some examples include:

- A computer that is not working
- A broken projector
- A damaged laboratory light
- A water leak
- A faulty classroom door
- A damaged socket
- Broken furniture
- Other facilities-related problems

5. Problems With the Current Approach:
The informal reporting approach can cause several problems.
First, a maintenance request can easily be forgotten or lost.
Second, there may be no clear way of deciding which problem should be fixed first. For example, a leaking water pipe may require more immediate attention than a broken chair.
Third, the person who reported a problem may not know whether the maintenance department has received or acted on the complaint.
It can also be difficult for management to determine how many problems are still unresolved or how long maintenance requests normally take to complete.
Finally, if the same problem occurs repeatedly, there may be no proper record that makes it easy to identify the pattern.

6. Proposed Solution:
CampusFix will provide a centralized system for managing university maintenance requests.
A student or staff member will be able to log into the system and report a problem by entering information such as the type of problem, its location and a description.
The request will then be recorded by the system. A maintenance supervisor can review the request, assign its priority and allocate it to an appropriate maintenance worker.
The maintenance worker can update the status of the request while working on it. Once the problem has been fixed, the worker can provide a resolution note and mark the request as resolved.
The request can then be closed after the required confirmation.
The basic workflow will therefore be:
Report → Record → Triage → Assign → Work → Resolve → Close

7. Scope of the Project:
The system will include;
- User login
- Different user roles
- Reporting of maintenance problems
- Recording the location of a problem
- Categorizing problems
- Setting priorities
- Assigning requests to maintenance staff
- Updating request status
- Recording resolution details
- Viewing previous requests
- Basic maintenance statistics

The system will not initially include:

- A mobile application
- Online payment
- Automatic purchasing of spare parts
- Integration with the university's existing student information system
- IoT sensors
- A full enterprise-level deployment

These features can be considered as possible future improvements.

8. Expected Outcome:
The expected outcome of CampusFix is a small working prototype that demonstrates how software engineering can be used to solve a real university problem.
The system should make it easier to report maintenance problems, keep track of their progress and determine whether they have been resolved.
The project will also demonstrate the software engineering process from requirements gathering and design to implementation, testing, documentation and maintenance.
